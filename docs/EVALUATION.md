# Model evaluation

Generated by `python evaluate.py` on 2026-09-25. Every number below comes from that script; rerun it after a game data update to refresh.

## 1. Banner forecast backtest

**Question:** how accurately can Global release dates be predicted from JP release history?

**Data:** 65 SSR banners (support + uma cards grouped by JP release date; GameTora). The 2 banners released together at Global launch (2025-06-26) are excluded from training, since that was a catalog dump, not a release decision.

**Method:** rolling-origin backtest. At each weekly cutoff from 2025-08-28 to 2026-09-10 (55 cutoffs), each model is fit only on releases known by that date and predicts every banner that actually released in the next 90 days. Models are compared on the first half of cutoffs (before 2026-03-05); the winner is chosen there and reported on the second half, which it never saw. The 80% interval is split-conformal: the 80th percentile of the winner's absolute errors on the tuning half.

| Model | Tuning MAE (days) | **Test MAE** | Test median | Test bias | Test 90th pct | All cutoffs MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed lag (median, baseline) | 47.0 | **76.7** | 77 | +76.7 | 89 | 60.6 |
| last lag (naive, baseline) | 18.9 | **16.0** | 15 | +15.9 | 29 | 17.6 |
| OLS, all history ✅ | 3.4 | **3.1** | 3 | -2.9 | 5 | 3.2 |
| OLS, last 30 | 3.4 | **2.6** | 2 | -2.1 | 5 | 3.0 |
| OLS, last 15 | 4.5 | **2.0** | 2 | -0.1 | 4 | 3.4 |
| OLS, last 8 | 4.3 | **3.4** | 3 | -0.0 | 7 | 3.9 |
| Theil-Sen, last 30 | 3.5 | **2.7** | 3 | -2.2 | 6 | 3.2 |
| Theil-Sen, last 15 | 4.6 | **1.9** | 2 | +0.3 | 4 | 3.4 |

**Result:** the model selected on the tuning half (*OLS, all history*) predicts release dates with a held-out mean error of **3.1 days** (95% CI 2.9-3.3) vs **16.0** (95% CI 14.5-17.2) for the strongest naive baseline (last observed lag, 81% lower) and 76.7 for a fixed lag, which fails because the JP→Global gap shrinks every month. Recency-windowed fits did slightly better on the test half than the selected model (2.6 days for the app's original last-30 fit): Global's pace drifted after the tuning period, which a window adapts to.

**Sample size, honestly:** the test half has 289 predictions, but they come from 28 overlapping weekly cutoffs about only **28 distinct banners**, so the effective sample is closer to the banner count. The CIs above resample whole cutoffs to respect that.

**Intervals:** a ±5 day interval covered **88%** of held-out releases (target 80%). The app's original interval averaged ±7.0 days and covered 100%: safe, but wider than it needed to be.

**Shipped in the app:** after testing, the model with the lowest error across all cutoffs, *OLS, last 30*, with a ±5 day interval (80th percentile of its backtest errors). That interval uses every cutoff, including the ones used to choose the model, so treat its coverage as slightly optimistic; the held-out coverage above is the honest check. Conformal coverage also assumes the future release pattern resembles the past, which a publisher can change at will.

Error by how far ahead the prediction was made (test half, MAE in days):

| Days ahead | OLS, all history | OLS, last 30 (original) | Fixed lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-30 days | 2.5 | 2.0 | 68.9 |
| 31-60 days | 3.0 | 2.5 | 77.1 |
| 61-90 days | 3.9 | 3.6 | 86.3 |

**Limitations:** predictions from neighbouring cutoffs overlap, so errors aren't independent and the effective sample is smaller than the prediction count. The Global schedule is set by the publisher and can change pace at any time (anniversaries, catch-up campaigns); a straight line can't anticipate that. Officially announced dates always override the forecast in the app.

## 2. Support card model vs expert tier list

**Question:** does the app's support-card score (an approximate training-contribution model built from each card's per-level effect table) rank cards the way experienced players do?

**Labels:** Game8's Global support card tier list (retrieved 2026-09-24; kept out of the public repo as third-party editorial content, so `data/tier_labels.json` must be regenerated locally to rerun this section): 158 Speed/Stamina/Power/Guts/Wit cards rated SS 5, S 39, A 71, B 37, C 6. Game8 ranks cards *within* each type, so every comparison is within type: 1571 card pairs of the same type and different tiers.

**Metric:** pairwise concordance, the share of those pairs the score orders the same way as Game8 (50% = coin flip, 100% = perfect). Model scores are at max limit break, matching Game8's assumption.

| Ranking | Concordance (all labeled cards) |
|---|---|
| Newer card is better (baseline) | 47.1% |
| SSR before SR, then newer (baseline) | 50.8% |
| **App model, hand-set weights** | **62.6%** |
| App model, shipped calibrated weights (fit on these same cards, so optimistic) | 74.4% |

Per type (default weights vs recency baseline):

| Type | Cards | Model | Recency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | 35 | 64.0% | 49.6% |
| Stamina | 28 | 62.0% | 48.9% |
| Power | 32 | 68.4% | 40.6% |
| Guts | 32 | 54.9% | 45.1% |
| Wit | 31 | 62.8% | 50.5% |

**Calibration (held out):** 300 random weight settings (each of 18 model constants scaled x0.5-x2) plus the defaults. For each of 20 stratified 50/50 splits of the cards, the best setting on the training half was scored on the held-out half. Probability-like constants are capped at 0.95.

| On held-out cards | Mean concordance ± SD |
|---|---|
| Recency baseline | 47.8% ± 4.1 |
| Hand-set weights | 62.1% ± 3.1 |
| Tuned on the other half | 72.3% ± 4.1 |

Tuned weights beat the hand-set ones on 20 of 20 held-out splits. Paired improvement: +10.1 points on average, smallest +4.4. The splits share cards, so they aren't independent: read the ± as spread across splits, not a confidence interval, and the 20/20 as consistency, not 20 separate confirmations.

**Error analysis:** 40 of the 158 cards have a *conditional* unique effect (e.g. a bonus that only applies under certain training conditions) that the model doesn't simulate yet. 5 of the 5 most-underrated cards are among them. On pairs of cards without one, the model agrees with Game8 **64.9%** of the time; on pairs involving one, only 59.6%. Modeling those effects is the clearest next improvement.

**Shipped:** the app now uses the setting fit on all labeled cards (`model.CALIBRATED`); the hand-set values remain in `model.WEIGHTS` as the starting point. The held-out numbers above are the honest estimate of how well it generalizes.

**Sensitivity:** widening the search to x0.25-x4 gives 73.2% held-out, but the fitted weights change a lot (e.g. `gain_main` x1.15 → x0.33). Many weight settings rank cards about equally well, so single weights aren't identifiable and shouldn't be interpreted. The gain is in the ranking, not in any one number.

**Limitations:** one expert source; its tiers are coarse and tuned to the current scenario, while the model is scenario-agnostic. Within-type labels mean the model's cross-type ordering (e.g. Speed vs Wit) isn't tested here. Cards Game8 doesn't rate aren't evaluated.
